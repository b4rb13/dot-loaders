### Complete free MIT v1 boundary

The entire approved v1 catalog stays free under MIT: native components and React adapter; composable skeleton primitives/multiline, text, chart, table, typing and region surfaces; all six approved chart idle silhouettes and line/bar/scatter handoffs; text scramble/pulse/lock/print; table type-on/shimmer, cell treatments and coordinated actual-row reveal; the remaining approved skeleton/typing/region/default motion; independent typed animation bindings and custom-authoring API; presets, essential styling/customization and pointer controls; accessibility and motion restrictions; SSR/CSP; performance improvements; complete docs and the standard playground.

Corrections and improvements to supported core functionality remain free under the already-approved maintenance policy. Core correctness, usability, accessibility, performance or user-authored animation support cannot depend on a paid pack. Distinctive v1 animation is not watered down for conversion. The approved source-informed repertoire is not assigned a paid tier. MIT permissions/notices and source/third-party provenance obligations remain intact.

### Optional paid value and rollout sequence

1. First validate one small composed-template collection: carefully designed application screens with complete loading, refresh, empty, error and responsive composition.
2. Genuinely additional authored motion/preset collections may follow, using the same public engine and extension contract. New collections are not permission to move approved core features behind payment.
3. Authoring/export tools are later investments requiring evidence; the standard free playground and examples remain usable.
4. Integration assistance, migrations, bespoke animations and general application work are separately scoped services, not automatic standard-purchase entitlements.

There is no private engine fork, hidden global provider, runtime license check or merchant dependency in consumer applications. Paid scope is additional creative/composed/tooling/service value. The initial implementation roadmap delivers the free library, docs/playground and release evidence independently of commerce. Discovery/pilot validation follows the eventual explicitly authorized free release; commerce readiness does not block free releases or maintenance.

No public price, subscription, checkout/accounts platform, merchant or SLA is selected. No paid product, lifetime-update promise or delivery schedule is announced by this resolution. Future artifacts must be accurately described and separately approved through their relevant gates.

### Licensing and source distribution

Commercial restrictions apply only to clearly identified, separately licensed paid assets. Bundled MIT and third-party files retain their own rights and notices. No blanket paid license overwrites the free package's permissions.

The approved paid-license policy intention permits the purchasing person/organization and project collaborators acting on its behalf to modify/use assets in own or client end products. Clients may receive and maintain delivered projects. Standalone resale, repackaging as another template/asset library or distribution of reusable paid source independently needs separate permission. General-purpose builders exporting the paid assets need a separate agreement. Exact wording for purchaser/team/client/source access and actual sales-region obligations is required pre-sale review; another vendor's terms are not adopted.

The first paid experiment delivers a versioned source archive with the license, dependency notices, setup instructions, changelog and tested compatibility. Customer builds do not require a private registry or merchant license server. Source can be retained locally; acquisition and update delivery are separate from application runtime. Normal disclosed third-party dependencies remain normal dependencies; no air-gapped or perpetual third-party availability guarantee is implied.

### Customer use, updates, downloads and support

| Entitlement | Approved policy |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase model | One-time purchase; no automatic subscription or renewal charge. |
| Acquired versions | Ongoing use and modification under the acquired terms after update/download access expires. |
| Update window | Twelve months from actual delivery for released collection updates and maintenance within the expressly advertised platform range. No promised feature cadence or implicit future framework-major compatibility. |
| Downloads | Original and entitled releases throughout that twelve-month window plus30 calendar days for downloads. Grace excludes releases after the update cutoff; publish dates/time zone and give an expiry reminder. |
| Renewal | Optional access to subsequent updates; existing acquired-file use is not made contingent on renewal. |
| Additional products | New collections are separate purchases unless expressly included. |
| Setup assistance | Thirty days of asynchronous documented setup assistance for one initial integration, starting at delivery, covering installation/configuration/advertised behavior. |
| Defects/maintenance | Reproducible supported collection defects follow maintenance through the update window. Customers may report issues afterward with honest support status. |
| Exclusions | No standard guaranteed response/resolution times, dedicated support channel, SLA, custom implementation or unlimited consulting. Publish supported versions and known limitations before purchase. |

Free-core maintenance follows its independent existing contract and remains free. Core fixes do not become paid collection updates. Neither continuing use nor a one-time purchase implies lifetime updates, indefinite hosted downloads or unlimited compatibility/support. Record the applicable promise per customer and honor it; later changes cannot silently reduce earlier commitments.

### Demand and bounded-pilot evidence

Require all initial discovery thresholds before proposing a paid pilot:

- Ten qualified prospect conversations about a precise proposed collection.
- Five independent prospects reporting substantially the same concrete problem and examples of its cost/work.
- Three distinct prospective buyers giving written, nonbinding purchase interest for a defined deliverable at a disclosed test price/scope.
- A bounded pilot plan with explicitly approved development budget, advertised compatibility, support allowance, delivery criteria and evaluation checkpoint.
- A conservative cost model including payment costs, delivery, maintenance, assistance and maintainer time, showing plausible positive contribution.

These are practical planning thresholds, not statistical proof, actual purchases or general product-market-fit evidence. Stars/downloads/compliments inform discovery, not purchasing proof; duplicates do not become independent buyers. The disclosed discovery test price is a hypothesis, not a public price fixed by this ticket.

Before accepting money require a reviewable deliverable, coherent offer/terms/delivery/update/support policies and actual-region refund/consumer-obligation plus relevant seller/payment/tax/data review. Actual paid use, completed and failed integrations, support effort and costs drive continue/revise/stop/expand decisions. Disappointing evidence causes revision or stopping the experiment while keeping the free product independent. Passing thresholds does not automatically launch checkout or authorize payments/outreach.

## Approved discontinuation and customer-access safeguards


1. **Keep acquired versions usable.** Expiry, ended sales, account loss or merchant-service shutdown does not add a runtime/build lock to acquired files or revoke their agreed continuing-use policy. Normal disclosed third-party dependencies remain separate; no promise of indefinite compatibility or an air-gapped dependency mirror.
2. **Finite delivery access.** Guarantee access to the original and entitled released versions throughout the twelve-month update window, plus30 calendar days afterward for downloading those entitled versions. The grace period does not grant updates released after the update cutoff. Publish cutoff dates/time zone, permit local copies and give an expiry reminder. No perpetual hosted-download promise.
3. **Planned retirement.** Stop new sales when withdrawal is decided, give affected customers at least60 calendar days' notice before a planned service retirement, and honor each existing customer's remaining update/support/download commitments even if they extend beyond that notice. The notice is not permission to shorten sold terms. A final eligible archive should include docs, notices, license, changelog and tested compatibility. No automatic public relicensing of paid files.
4. **Promises cannot be fulfilled.** If an emergency, rights/security issue or inability to maintain delivery prevents fulfillment, stop affected sales, inform affected buyers promptly and provide the reviewed remedy for the actual obligation, including refund/replacement where appropriate. Protect customers from a compromised download; the planned notice period must not force continued distribution of an unsafe/unlicensed artifact. Exact remedies/consumer obligations are reviewed before sale, not invented afterward.
5. **Recover access without altering customer code.** Provide a documented recovery route using verified purchase/organization authority, with no mandatory merchant identity or secret in the consuming application. Recovery must not expose another customer's files, purchase details or broaden a client's licensed source rights.
6. **Preserve the purchased promise.** Record the delivered version, applicable license/compatibility/update/support/download terms and entitlement dates with each order. Later policy changes cannot silently reduce earlier commitments. Provide a minimal entitlement/receipt export; protect retained customer records under the actual pre-sale data policy. No consumer telemetry or mandatory call-home is added to library code.

These are approved product-policy commitments for a future offering, not statutory notice/refund conclusions or a finalized sales agreement. Capacity, delivery continuity and actual sales-region legal review must be established before accepting money. Discontinuation does not move any approved free feature behind a paywall or make paid assets necessary to use the core.

## Six approved commercial validation gates

1. **Free-product independence.** Packed free native/React consumers, current approved motion, docs/playground and custom authoring work without any paid import, account, entitlement, seller service or runtime check. The free release is not contingent on commercial readiness. MIT notices/provenance and the existing maintenance policy remain intact.
2. **Demand and bounded economics.** Retain evidence for the10/5/3 discovery thresholds, qualification/deduplication and one precise offering/test price. State uncertainty and distinguish intent from purchases. Approve the experiment's scope, development budget, compatibility/support capacity, cost assumptions and evaluation checkpoint before spending/charging. Costs include fees, delivery, maintenance, assistance and maintainer time; social metrics alone cannot pass.
3. **Rights, terms and honest offer.** Before payment, review original/third-party provenance and actual sales-region terms, buyer/client/source scope, updates/downloads/support, refund/consumer requirements and relevant seller/payment/tax/data obligations. Preserve the applicable promise per order. Product page, sample, license, receipt and delivered package must agree. Review demand by showing a concrete deliverable; no misleading availability, update or support claim.
4. **Delivered quality and compatibility.** Test the exact versioned archive in clean native/React/Next fixtures as applicable, with setup/license/notices/changelog and advertised states/platform range. The public engine is shared: no private fork, hidden provider or unreviewed integration. Existing accessibility/readiness/motion/security/cleanup contracts remain binding. Free package closures retain their numeric ceilings; separately imported paid assets declare and validate their incremental delivery/runtime cost before any claim. Aggregate runtime resource caps still apply. Packaging cannot hide a free-core regression.
5. **Entitlement, access and continuity.** Demonstrate correct delivery dates/cutoffs, eligible updates, grace downloads, authorized account recovery and local copies. Test expiry/retirement/provider outage and removal of scoped access without breaking acquired projects or leaking other customers' data. Establish capacity to honor every sold window and the documented emergency/remedy route. Selling stops if safe authorized delivery cannot be assured.
6. **Pilot evaluation and expansion approval.** Retain actual paid-usage/integration, issue/support-time, delivery/refund and cost evidence at the preapproved checkpoint. Compare against the scoped success/stop criteria; disclose shortfalls. Explicit owner review decides continue/revise/stop or expand. Passing discovery is not a sale authorization, pilot sales are not general product-market-fit proof, and no result makes core maintenance dependent on commerce.

Gates are required before the corresponding future activity. Unavailable evidence stays unverified. Commercial delivery may not waive the existing core's safety/accessibility/readiness/identity/stale-work/cleanup guarantees. This planning ticket approves policy and future gates; it does not perform outreach, collect customer data/money, select a merchant, set a public price, implement checkout or deploy/publish anything.
